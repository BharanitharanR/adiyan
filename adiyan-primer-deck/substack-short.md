# Substack short version

I'm building Adiyan — an AI agent harness that runs on your own machine, not someone else's cloud.

The part I'm most excited to talk about: it's peer-to-peer. If your machine is ever too busy or too small to answer something, Adiyan can ask a peer's Adiyan for help — but only when you explicitly opt one message into it, never by default. No cloud provider, no marketplace, no metered API bill. Just machines helping each other when asked, the same way you'd borrow sugar from a neighbor and expect to lend it back someday.

It's not a pitch deck yet, though — it's already working. Adiyan Reader is the proof: hand it a book, and it reads you a page every night as a real WhatsApp voice note, then quizzes you the next morning on what stuck. It runs on its own schedule, unattended, today.

The cloud centralizes AI. Adiyan distributes it — and it already does something real while it's at it.

There's an older idea underneath this that I keep coming back to. Gandhi's swaraj wasn't just political independence — it was the insistence that self-rule has to start at the level of the individual, or the village, before it means anything at scale. Adiyan's answer to "where should your AI run" is the same instinct: on your own machine, under your own control, first. And swadeshi wasn't isolation — it was using what you and your neighbors already had before reaching for something imported. That's exactly what the peer network is: not a marketplace, not a subscription, just consenting neighbors lending each other spare capacity, the same capacity going the other way when the need reverses. Self-reliant first, cooperative by choice — not because a platform requires it, but because that's what neighbors do.
