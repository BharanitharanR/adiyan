Why one moment of sound needs exactly 7 numbers

Forget "codebooks" for a second. Think of it as describing one instant of a sound three times, at three levels of zoom - and you need all three descriptions before you can actually draw the picture.

Imagine describing a single video frame to someone over the phone. First you say "a face" - one word, the coarse shape. Then, twice as precisely, you say "eyes... mouth" - two words, roughly where the features are. Then, four times as precisely, you say "left eyebrow... right eyebrow... upper lip... lower lip" - four words, the fine detail. 1 + 2 + 4 = 7 words to describe one frame, at three zoom levels stacked on top of each other. That's exactly what SNAC does for one instant of sound, except the "words" are numbers, and the "frame" is about 21 milliseconds of audio.

Watch the 7 numbers arrive, one at a time

Orpheus emits them in a fixed order. Step through it.

Coarse (1 number)
Medium (2 numbers)
Fine (4 numbers)
Why this matters for "drags or muffles": you cannot decode with only 5 of these 7 - the medium and fine numbers are refinements of the coarse one, not independent measurements. If Orpheus stops generating mid-way through a group of 7 (say, after only 4 arrive), those 4 numbers describe an incomplete picture and get thrown away rather than turned into wrong-sounding audio. That loss is real, but it's small - about 21 milliseconds, a fraction of one syllable - which is why it's probably not the main cause of what you're hearing. The real culprit is more likely visible now in ~/.Adiyan/logs/llm_calls.log, where the actual token sequence for any bad sentence is logged in full.
