# What is Adiyan — the catchy cut

A shot-by-shot script: what to show on screen, what to say. Built around the website's four offerings, in order. Total run time roughly 90 seconds if you keep pace tight — cut a line rather than slow down.

---

### 1. Hook (0:00–0:08)
**Show:** black screen or your face, no visuals yet.
**Say:**
> "Adiyan" means "your humble servant." Not "your subscription." Not "your API bill." Your servant — running on your machine, doing what you tell it.

### 2. The problem, fast (0:08–0:18)
**Show:** a generic "AI chatbot" screen, then an arrow pointing off to "the cloud."
**Say:**
> Every AI tool you've used works the same way — your words leave your machine, get processed on someone else's, and come back. You don't own it. You rent it, one message at a time.

### 3. The turn (0:18–0:26)
**Show:** cut to the Adiyan website hero — the harness diagram loading in.
**Say:**
> Adiyan doesn't do that. It's an agent harness you install on your own computer. One command, and it's running — locally, on your machine.

### 4. Offering 1 — Harness (0:26–0:38)
**Show:** the "seven agents" block diagram (Orchestrator + the six agents around it).
**Say:**
> Out of the box, you get seven agents already talking to each other — one that routes your messages, one that remembers you, one that runs jobs on schedule. All wired in. All local. You open WhatsApp — that's the whole interface.

### 5. Offering 2 — Reader (0:38–0:50)
**Show:** the Reader flow diagram (upload → pages → voice note → quiz).
**Say:**
> Hand it a book. Adiyan Reader splits it into pages, reads you one every night as a voice note, and quizzes you the next day. A reading habit that runs itself — and actually holds you to it.

### 6. Offering 3 — Dev Kit (0:50–1:02)
**Show:** the dev-kit diagram (Your new agent → Agent Registry → Orchestrator).
**Say:**
> Know a little Python? Build your own agent, register it once, and Orchestrator finds it automatically. No routing code. No plumbing. It just shows up.

### 7. Offering 4 — Community (1:02–1:14)
**Show:** the peer-to-peer diagram (your machine ↔ a peer's machine).
**Say:**
> And if your machine's ever too busy or too small — Adiyan can ask a peer's Adiyan for help. Only if you say yes. Every single time.

### 8. The line that sells it (1:14–1:22)
**Show:** the closing slide — "Register me to make my life better."
**Say:**
> The cloud centralizes AI. Adiyan distributes it. Not another AI subscription — a different way to own one.

### 9. Close / CTA (1:22–1:30)
**Show:** the install command on screen, big and legible.
**Say:**
> One command. Your machine. Your servant. Link's in the next video — or right below.

---

## Delivery notes

- Say it like you mean it, not like you're reading a script — the whole point is it doesn't sound AI-generated, so don't let the delivery sound like a script either.
- Cut on the beat: change the visual the instant the line changes, don't let a shot sit static through two lines.
- If 90 seconds feels tight while filming, cut section 2 (the problem) short rather than rushing the four offerings — those are the substance, the problem framing is just the hook.
- The four offering lines are deliberately parallel in rhythm (short setup, short payoff) — keep that pattern if you adapt any of them.
